# Enzymatic Greywater Treatment System  
### A Decentralized, Low-Cost Biotechnological Approach  

---

## 1. Problem Statement  

Greywater reuse has emerged as a critical strategy in addressing global water scarcity, particularly in rapidly urbanizing and water-stressed regions such as India. Despite its potential, the implementation of greywater recycling systems remains limited due to technological, economic, and infrastructural constraints.

A major challenge lies in the lack of simple and cost-effective treatment systems that can be deployed at the point of generation. Advanced technologies such as membrane bioreactors, although highly efficient in removing organic load and pathogens, require significant capital investment, skilled operation, and ongoing maintenance, making them unsuitable for decentralized deployment (Fikri et al., 2023).

In addition, many conventional treatment approaches rely heavily on chemical processes such as coagulation and oxidation. While effective, these methods introduce secondary environmental concerns, including sludge generation and potential toxic byproducts (Al-Maqdi et al., 2021).

Furthermore, the centralized nature of wastewater treatment infrastructure does not align with the decentralized generation of greywater. Since greywater is produced at the household level, transporting it to centralized facilities is inefficient and often impractical (Li et al., 2009).

Taken together, these challenges highlight the absence of a low-cost, environmentally safe, and decentralized greywater treatment system operating at source.

## 2. Literature Review  

### 2.1 Enzyme-Based Wastewater Treatment  

Enzymatic treatment utilizes biological catalysts such as lipases, proteases, and amylases to degrade organic contaminants into simpler compounds. These enzymes target fats, proteins, and carbohydrates, respectively, enabling efficient breakdown of greywater pollutants.

Experimental studies have demonstrated that enzyme mixtures can achieve significant reductions in biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) and chemical oxygen demand (COD), often exceeding 90% under optimized conditions (Nazim et al., 2025). These systems are advantageous due to their specificity, biodegradability, and low energy requirements.

However, enzyme activity is influenced by environmental factors such as pH, temperature, and the presence of surfactants commonly found in greywater. Challenges related to enzyme stability and real-world applicability remain significant (Al-Maqdi et al., 2021).

---

### 2.2 Existing Greywater Purification Techniques  

Greywater treatment technologies are broadly categorized into physical, chemical, and biological systems. Physical methods such as sand filtration are widely used due to their simplicity but are limited in removing dissolved contaminants. Chemical methods provide higher removal efficiencies but introduce environmental concerns and operational complexity.

Biological treatment systems, particularly membrane bioreactors, are considered the benchmark for high-quality effluent, achieving removal efficiencies greater than 90% for BOD and COD (Fikri et al., 2023). However, their cost and maintenance requirements limit their applicability in decentralized settings.

Overall, existing systems demonstrate strong performance under controlled conditions but lack accessibility and scalability for household-level deployment (Li et al., 2009).

---

### 2.3 Chemical vs Biological Methods  

Chemical methods typically provide rapid and efficient contaminant removal but may result in environmental and operational drawbacks such as sludge generation and chemical dependency. In contrast, biological methods are more environmentally sustainable and adaptable to decentralized applications, though they may require longer treatment times and controlled conditions (Al-Maqdi et al., 2021).

## 3. Proposed Methodology  

The proposed system introduces a decentralized enzymatic treatment approach comprising enzymatic tablets and microbial bioaugmentation.

The primary method involves the formulation of enzyme tablets containing lipase, protease, and amylase, combined with stabilizers and binders to ensure controlled release and preservation of activity. Upon introduction into greywater, these tablets dissolve and release enzymes that catalyze the breakdown of organic matter, leading to reductions in BOD, COD, turbidity, and oil content.

The treatment process is conducted under ambient conditions, making it suitable for real-world deployment without specialized infrastructure. Performance is evaluated using standard analytical methods, including BOD, COD, turbidity, and pH.

To enhance efficiency, a secondary bioaugmentation strategy is employed using *Bacillus subtilis*, a non-pathogenic microorganism capable of producing hydrolytic enzymes and biosurfactants. The bacteria are immobilized in sodium alginate beads, enabling sustained enzyme production and improved degradation of complex pollutants.

This combined approach creates a synergistic system that enhances treatment efficiency and stability over time.

---

## 4. Expected Outcomes  

The proposed system is expected to achieve substantial reductions in key water quality parameters, including BOD, COD, turbidity, and oil and grease content. Based on existing studies, removal efficiencies in the range of 70–90% are anticipated under optimized conditions (Nazim et al., 2025).

The integration of microbial bioaugmentation is expected to further enhance performance by enabling continuous enzyme production and improving degradation of complex organic compounds.

---

## 5. Significance of the Study  

This study addresses critical gaps in existing greywater treatment technologies by proposing a low-cost, decentralized, and environmentally sustainable solution. The system eliminates reliance on harmful chemicals and reduces infrastructure requirements, making it suitable for household and small-scale applications.

By integrating enzymatic treatment with microbial bioaugmentation, the proposed approach offers a scalable and practical alternative to conventional treatment systems.

---

## 6. Conclusion  

Greywater treatment technologies currently face limitations related to cost, complexity, and environmental impact. Enzymatic treatment provides a promising alternative, offering specificity, efficiency, and sustainability.

The proposed enzymatic tablet system, combined with microbial bioaugmentation, represents a novel approach to decentralized greywater treatment. With further validation and optimization, this system has the potential to bridge the gap between laboratory research and real-world implementation.
