### 1. Rotary Vacuum Filter (Filtration)

This is a guaranteed 5-mark question. You must be able to:

- Draw and explain the design and working principle of a continuous rotary vacuum filter.
    
- Write the correlation for cycle time and filtration time.
    
- Derive the linearized equation establishing the relation between $t/V$ and $V$.
    

### 2. Ultrasonication Kinetics (Cell Disruption)

The exact same baseline numerical problem appeared in both 2024 and 2025: calculating the time required for 90% intracellular antibiotic recovery from a bacterial cell suspension, given that 50% is recovered in 40 minutes.

- Master the first-order kinetics formula for this problem.
    
- In 2025, they added constraints regarding temperature increases ($5^{\circ}C$ per 30 mins) and enzyme denaturation (above $45^{\circ}C$), so be prepared to factor those in.
    

### 3. Relative Centrifugal Force (RCF) & Settling Velocity

Centrifugation math is a staple in these exams. You should intimately know:

- How to define RCF and calculate it using RPM and rotor radius.
    
- How altering parameters (like doubling the radius or changing viscosity/particle diameter) affects the centrifugal force or terminal velocity.
    
- How to calculate settling velocity for cells in a tubular bowl centrifuge and understand how Stokes' Law applies to centrifugation.
    

### 4. High-Pressure Homogenization

This cell disruption technique is tested for both its theory and mathematical applications.

- Understand the key principle and underlying equations.
    
- Be able to plot relative cell disruption versus increasing pressure/number of passes.
    
- Know how to calculate flow velocity and dynamic pressure given the flow rate, nozzle diameter, and density.
    

### 5. Protein Precipitation & "Salting Out"

This is the core of the CO4 (Course Outcome 4) questions.

- Understand the interactive forces involved and the role of the Stern layer in protein precipitation.
    
- Be able to scientifically explain why a protein might remain soluble at a lower saturation (e.g., 30% ammonium sulfate) but precipitate at a higher saturation (e.g., 60%).
    
- Know the terms "salting in" and "salting out".
