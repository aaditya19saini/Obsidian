# Definition: 
Absorption is the process where a drug moves from its site of administration directly into the systemic circulation.

### Bioavailability
- the rate and extent of a drug's absorption.
- The bioavailability of a drug from its dosage form dictates the onset, intensity, and duration of its therapeutic action.

# Mechanism of drug absorption 
## Passive Diffusion 
(Major process)
properties:
1. energy independent process 
2. based on concentration gradient 
3. Follow FICK's LAW

Depends on: 
1. Depends on the area and thickness of membrane 
2. depends on molecular weight of drugs 
3. Depends on Lipid solubility of drugs 
4. Sufficient aqueous solubility of drugs 

Transport of drugs depends on:
1. pH of the absorption site (GI pH) 
2. Dissociation constant pKa 
3. Lipid solubility of the unionized drugs 
4. The % of unionized form of drug at a particular pH 
5. Partition coefficient of the drug • Molecular weight of the drug

For eg: Testosterone, cortisol, Phenobarbital 

## Active Transport 
Natural substances having higher molecular weight and low lipid solubility gets transported by spending energy. 
- they form complexes with carrier molecules to get transported (Carrier Mediated)
Eg:  LEVODOPA, METHOTREXATE , Penicillin

## Facilitated Diffusion 
- This is a carrier-mediated transport that relies purely on a concentration gradient and requires no energy. 
- While it's how Vitamin B12 is absorbed, it's not a significant absorption mechanism for most drugs

## Ionic or electrochemical diffusion 
This transport depends on concentration gradients, potential differences, or electrical gradients across the membrane. Permeation speed ranks from unionized molecules (fastest) to anions, to cations
## Pore Transport 
Water-soluble, low-molecular-weight drugs pass through the membrane's aqueous channels, driven by hydrostatic pressure differences
## Endocytosis 
Involves the cell membrane invaginating to form a sac around the material. This includes pinocytosis and phagocytosis, which are relatively slow and inefficient but are used to absorb large proteins or the oral polio vaccine


# Factors Governing Drug Absorption

- **Physicochemical Factors related to drugs:** 
   1. Drug absorption is generally limited by its **dissolution rate**. 
   2. Most drugs are weak acids or weak bases, and the percentage of the unionized (absorbable) form depends on the drug's dissociation constant (pKa) and the pH of the environment. 
   3. ==Weakly acidic drugs are more unionized in the stomach==, while **weakly basic drugs are more unionized in the small intestine**. Higher lipid solubility and partition coefficients lead to better absorption.

| Factor                                     | Relationship to Absorption                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lipid solubility**                       | More lipid-soluble drugs cross cell membranes easily (since membranes are lipoidal). High lipid solubility → better passive diffusion. |
| **Dissociation constant (pKa)**            | Determines the proportion of unionized vs. ionized drug at a given pH. Unionized forms are absorbed more readily.                      |
| **pH-partition hypothesis**                | Weak acids absorb better in acidic stomach (more unionized), weak bases absorb better in alkaline intestine.                           |
| **Drug solubility & dissolution rate**     | A drug must dissolve before absorption. Poor solubility or slow dissolution → reduced absorption.                                      |
| **Crystal form**                           | Amorphous forms dissolve faster than crystalline forms. Polymorphs differ in solubility → affect bioavailability.                      |
| **Particle size & effective surface area** | Smaller particles → larger surface area → faster dissolution → better absorption.                                                      |
| **Complexation**                           | Complex formation with other molecules can either enhance (e.g., cyclodextrin complexes) or reduce absorption depending on stability.  |
| **Viscosity of medium**                    | Higher viscosity in GI fluids slows diffusion and drug movement → reduces absorption rate.                                             |
| **Drug stability in GIT**                  | Drugs unstable in acidic pH (e.g., penicillin G) or degraded by enzymes have poor oral bioavailability.                                |
| **Salt forms of drug**                     | Salt forms usually have higher aqueous solubility → faster dissolution → improved absorption compared to free base/acid.               |





















- **Pharmaceutical Factors:** The dosage form drastically impacts bioavailability.
  The text ranks bioavailability from highest to lowest: `Solutions > Emulsions > Suspensions > Capsules > Tablets > Coated tablets > Enteric coated tablets > Sustained release products.`
    
- **Patient-Related Factors:** Absorption is influenced by individual physiological states such as gastric emptying speed, intestinal transit time, GI pH, specific disease states, and interactions with GI contents like food or other drugs
